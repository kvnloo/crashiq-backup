- **Gained** = crashes off the map that the tier would place. The headline is crashes with no usable GPS, the only ones
  the app sends to these tiers.
- **verified_typo** runs inside the locator, so the statewide run records it per crash. A misspelled name never meets
  the other street as written, so there is no self-consistent truth for it (as for near and fuzzy in the
  difficult-bin report): it's compared with exact on all police GPS. On open data it matches exact, but on 26 and 50
  crashes the low bound can't reach the bar.
- **postmile and the crossings** run outside the locator, on a corridor. Statewide (`run11_tiers.py`) the corridor is
  the report's other road: postmile uses the route it names by number; crossings use every OSM way of that name in
  the city box + 1 km (else the county), with railways and named paths from the same OSM extract
  (`crossing_features.py`). Truth = police GPS that fits the report, hidden, self-consistent (crossings: within 400 ft
  of the railway / path too). Scored as a point within 250 ft.
- **postmile parse defect (for Engineering):** `mile_marker.marker` takes the first number after MM, so the CHP form
  "MM <route> <county> <pm>" ("MM 58 KER 0.50") is read as postmile 58: 7 of the 222 tested reports, all over half a
  mile off. It doesn't change the call; the rest is far below the bar too. CHP mile markers may also be signed miles,
  not postmiles (as `mile_marker.py` warns): the median miss is 510 ft.
- **Crossings:** 7,021 path and 3,801 rail reports statewide, but most have fitting GPS; of the 969 off the map, most
  name a street not on the map, a street that doesn't cross the feature, or a mapped street called "Railroad Ave"
  (left to the locator).
