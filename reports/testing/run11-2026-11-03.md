Notes:
- Gained = crashes run 11 leaves off the map that the tier would place. The headline is crashes with no usable GPS, the only ones the app sends to these tiers (crashes.pull routes no-GPS reports); "GPS off its roads" is shown apart.
- verified_typo runs inside the locator, so the statewide run records it per crash (`tier` column). Its hidden-GPS figure is right intersection on all police GPS: a misspelled name never meets the other street as written, so it has no self-consistent truth (as near and fuzzy in the difficult-bin report); the exact tier is compared on the same all-GPS measure (its 92% is on self-consistent GPS).
- postmile and the crossings run outside the locator, on a corridor. Statewide (`run11_tiers.py`) the corridor is the report's other road: for postmile the route it names by number (no number, no spot); for crossings every OSM way of that name in the city box + 1 km (else the county), with railways and named paths from the same OSM extract (`crossing_features.py`). A whole street crosses more often than a drawn stretch, so the crossing gain is a floor. Truth = police GPS that fits the report, hidden; scored as a point (they place a point, not an intersection), self-consistent only (crossings: GPS also within 400 ft of the railway / path). Bar: the exact tier's right-intersection low bound on self-consistent GPS; its final-point figure on all GPS is shown in brackets (noisier truth, a lower bar).
- postmile reads the first number after MM as the postmile, so the CHP form "MM <route> <county> <pm>" ("MM 58 KER 0.50") is read as postmile 58: 7 of the 222 tested reports, 7 of them over half a mile off. A parse defect for Engineering; it doesn't change the call (the rest is far below the bar too).
- SF DataSF and LAPD open data: these reports are city streets; mile markers and railway / trail reports barely occur there, so the open-data tests can't speak for postmile or the crossings.
