# Going Nationwide Means One Engine, Many Data Adapters and Per-State Study Rules

Issue #4 · Hermes · researched 2026-10-18 · status: research only, nothing here is decided

CrashIQ can go national. The data and rules it depends on, though, are state-by-state. The federal layers cover every state but cannot drive a corridor study on their own. FARS has coordinates for every fatal crash and no other severities. CRSS is a national sample with no usable locations. HPMS has AADT on federal-aid roads but not on local streets. What does carry over nationally is the method layer: KABCO, the HSM, the CMF Clearinghouse (bulk-downloadable), and the SS4A action-plan requirement. SS4A asks for the same safety analysis and high-injury network in every state, which makes it the most portable study mode CrashIQ could build. Everything a study actually needs beyond that is state-specific: record-level crashes with coordinates, crash costs, countermeasure lists, HSIP scoring and ICE procedures. Access varies widely. **Massachusetts** (open ArcGIS API, KABCO, 93–97% geocoded, AADT on every crash record) and **Pennsylvania** (public downloads for 2006–2025 with decimal lat/long and KABCO) are as open as California's CCRS, or close to it. **Texas** is public but needs registration for bulk files. **New York** outside NYC and **Washington** keep geocoded record-level data behind agency-sponsored logins or emailed requests. **Florida** has good data, and a strong free state tool (Signal Four Analytics) already does much of what CrashIQ would. Almost no state publishes a licence that clearly allows commercial reuse. That, more than the engineering, is what sets the rollout pace.

**Method.** Two research sub-agents covered (A) federal layers, CA, NY, MA and NJ and (B) TX, FL and WA, plus a nine-state screen. They read official pages, PDFs and live APIs on 2026-10-18. Hermes spot-checked the key numbers against live APIs the same night: MassDOT 2026 layer = 95,962 crashes; NYC Open Data max crash date = 2026-06-11; data.ny.gov crash years = 2021–2024; PennDOT 2026 data dictionary reachable; LRSM v1.8 text says "82 countermeasures". Anything not confirmed from a primary source is marked **UNVERIFIED**. Working notes with every source URL: `research_notes/state-research-2026-10-18/`.

**Federal programs every state runs.** HSIP (23 USC 148, 23 CFR 924) with an SHSP. A Vulnerable Road User Safety Assessment: if VRU deaths are ≥15% of the total, ≥15% of HSIP goes to VRU safety ([FHWA](https://highways.dot.gov/sites/fhwa.dot.gov/files/2024-07/HSIP%20VRU%20Fact%20Sheet_508.pdf)). FHWA LRSP guidance ([FHWA](https://highways.dot.gov/safety/proven-safety-countermeasures/local-road-safety-plans)). And **SS4A action plans**, whose safety-analysis component asks for a baseline of fatal and serious-injury crashes by location, severity, contributing factors and crash type, on "all roadways… without regard for ownership," and "a geospatial identification of higher-risk locations… (a high-injury network or equivalent)" ([USDOT](https://www.transportation.gov/grants/ss4a/comprehensive-safety-action-plans)). That is one specification for all 50 states, and it maps directly onto CrashIQ's profile and findings.

**Legal layer, same everywhere.** Data "compiled or collected for the purpose of identifying, evaluating, or planning the safety enhancement of potential accident sites" under §148 can't be used in discovery or as evidence ([23 USC 407](https://law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/23/407)). WA, CO, VA and FL all attach this notice to their crash releases (state sections below). DPPA (18 USC 2721) limits use of personal information from DMV records, which matters in NY and VA ([LII](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/18/2721)). Hermes's reading, not legal advice: CrashIQ exports for HSIP or safety planning should carry a §407 notice, and agency data-use agreements are likely to forbid resale.

**Competitors that span states.** AASHTOWare Safety (Numetric) is licensed by state DOTs at $100k–$350k per module per year. CA, FL, TX and NY are in the top price group ([AASHTO](https://aashtoware.org/products/safety/safety-ordering)), and NJ and GA already run their public crash dashboards on it. Citian CRASH is the AI-native competitor (SCAG) ([Citian](https://citian.co/crash)). LexisNexis BuyCrash sells individual report PDFs, not analytics ([LexisNexis](https://risk.lexisnexis.com/products/lexisnexis-buycrash)).

## What CrashIQ must make state-specific, and what it can share

**Shared engine (build once):**
- **Canonical crash schema** modelled on MMUCC: crash / unit / person, KABCO severity, one count per crash at its worst outcome, plus a `state` and `source` field on every record and the existing location provenance (`gps/exact/fuzzy/jev(p)/user/unmatched`).
- Geocoding pipeline (OSM intersections, name normalizer, candidate list, Jev choice). Alias tables stay per state and city as versioned files.
- Corridor drawing, crash assignment, profile tables, findings contract and validator, audit block, export framework.
- Method library: HSM rate formulas, threshold-proportion test, EB (v2), CMF Clearinghouse engine, FHWA pattern library.
- National overlays: FARS fatal crashes, HPMS AADT where present.
- **SS4A / HIN action-plan mode.** One federal specification, valid in every state.

**State pack (one per state, versioned data files, nothing hard-coded):**
- **Data adapter:** how to fetch (live API, bulk file, or user upload), field mapping to the canonical schema, the **severity map** (NY's Fatal/Injury/PD, Seattle's 3/2b/2/1/0 and TX numeric codes don't map one-to-one onto KABCO, so each needs a documented rule), reporting threshold, PDO caveats, location referencing (NY reference markers, TX/FL milepost), coordinate coverage note, legal notices (§407, DPPA, state confidentiality).
- **Method tables:** crash costs (NY Red Book, CA HSIP Analyzer, FDOT FDM 122.6.2, TxDOT NSC), countermeasure list (CA's CRF list, TX Work Codes, MassDOT CMF list, or the Clearinghouse in NY), default data windows (CA HSIP 3–5 years, TX HSIP 3, TX TSP 5).
- **Study modes:** CA HSIP / ISOAP / ATP; TX TSP safety section + SII; MA RSA packet + ICE + Safety Alternatives Analysis; WA City Safety + LRSP 7-step + ICE; FL ICE + PD&E. A state's modes ship only when golden-tested against a real study in that state's format.

**Two adapter types.** Open states (CA, MA, PA, VA, NYC, TX after registration) get a live or scheduled pull. Gated states (NY CLEAR, WSDOT requests, FL S4, OH GCAT) get an **import of the user's own export**: the user brings data they are entitled to, and CrashIQ never redistributes it. The same importer also covers licence-unclear states until written terms exist.

**Implication for the build now (no change made).** The drafted C3 issue (#9, `ccrs`) pulls CCRS directly. Shaping its output as the canonical schema, with `state='CA'` from day one, is cheap now and expensive to retrofit. This is Q2 below.

## Conclusion

CrashIQ's engine can be national, but its value comes from state packs. Each one is a data adapter plus that state's method tables and study formats, golden-tested against a real study. MA and TX can follow CA with the least new method risk, PA and VA are the open-data additions, and NY and WA need the user-export path. The cheapest move that keeps all of this open is a design decision, not code: make the v1 data model state-neutral before C3 is built. The most important open item is licensing, which no amount of engineering removes. Methodology correctness comes before speed: no state should ship until its severity map, crash costs and countermeasure list are written as versioned files and checked against a real study in that state.
