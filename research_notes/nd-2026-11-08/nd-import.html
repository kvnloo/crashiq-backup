North Dakota: every crash, 2017–2025

In one line: last week's screen said North Dakota was request-only. It isn't: NDDOT's crashes sit on a public map layer (hosted by NDSU for NDDOT), every one with a map point and a K A B C O level. No street names, and no licence that says a paid tool may use it.

1. What North Dakota has

WhatExample
Crashes9,800–15,100 a year (2024: 9,803, the same as NDDOT's own Crash Summary).
Map pointEvery crash, all inside the state.
Injury levelPer crash: K A B C O (2017–2025: 825 fatal, 3,040 serious).
Street namesNone. Not needed to place, but the report can't name the streets.
MissingCrash costs per level, statewide average rates, drivers and vehicles.

3. A real corridor

13th Avenue South, 45th to 25th Street, Fargo, 1.95 mi, 2020–2024: 500 crashes: 2 fatal, 13 serious, 105 minor, 108 possible, 272 property damage. 59 interstate crashes at the I-29 interchange were left out, as they should be. No dollar total (NDDOT publishes no crash costs). PDF and Excel exports work.

What you need to do

  1. Ask NDDOT's Highway Safety Division (and NDSU's DOT Support Center, which hosts the layer) in writing if a paid tool may read it. NDDOT's manual has cities promise not to pass on crash data it emails them; the public layer has no such notice. If you'd rather wait, the pack flips to "not offered" with one line.
  2. Decide on crash costs: NDDOT has none per injury level.

What we didn't check

The licence with NDDOT; a second live NDDOT view with more rows than the official count; Fargo or Bismarck city data (none found).
