Maine: crash data for CrashIQ

Hermes, 8 Nov 2026. Measured live today. Full notes: me-import.md, methods: me-methods.md.

Short answer: Maine works. MaineDOT puts every crash since 2016 online, free, no login, with a map point and KABCO severity. It's live in CrashIQ as a ready state. The catches are the licence for a paid tool and no street names.

1. What Maine has

  • MaineDOT crash layer: 359,187 crashes, Jan 2016 to 22 Oct 2026, about 33,000 to 35,000 a year. Example: 2024 has 32,728.
  • Map point on all but 4. MaineDOT places each crash on its own road network. Example: Cumberland County 2024, 7,818 of 7,818 have a point.
  • KABCO severity, plus a count of people at each level. Example: a Forest Ave crash with one B injury is "B".
  • No street names. Each crash gives a route code instead. Example: "US 302" (Forest Ave), or "RD INV 05 60621" for a local street. So CrashIQ places crashes by MaineDOT's point only. Every crash has one, so nothing is lost.
  • No driver or vehicle tables, so collision diagrams are empty.
  • Parking lots, driveways and private roads aren't in it (MaineDOT's own note).

3. Maine's own methods

  • High Crash Location = 8 or more crashes in 3 years and Critical Rate Factor over 1.0 (MaineDOT rule Ch. 305, page 18). CrashIQ shows this rule but still screens with the national HSM critical rate.
  • At least 3 years of crashes (same page). CrashIQ uses this.
  • No MaineDOT crash cost table. MaineDOT's grant paperwork uses USDOT's costs (K $13.7M, 2024 $).

What you need to do

  1. Ask MaineDOT for a written OK to show its crash data in a paid tool. The only text is "Users must assume responsibility in determining the usability of this data."
  2. Say yes or no to using USDOT crash costs for Maine.

What we didn't check

  • How ramps are coded (this matters near Interstate exits).
  • Accuracy of MaineDOT's points. There is nothing independent to compare them with.
