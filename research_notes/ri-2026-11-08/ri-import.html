Rhode Island: public by law, but only on request

In June 2026 Rhode Island passed a law that makes RIDOT's crash data a public record. But RIDOT still hasn't put it online. You have to ask for it with a public-records request. So there's no Rhode Island pack yet.

What we found

  • The data exists and uses the right injury levels. In 2025 RIDOT recorded 36,850 crashes: 0.1% fatal, 0.6% serious, 2.5% minor, 15.7% possible injury and 75.6% no injury.
  • The "Crash Data" links on RIDOT's site are summary PDFs, not crash lists.
  • No open map layer. We searched RIDOT's own online map account (65 items) and the state GIS server. They hold street sweeping, litter and bridges, but no crashes.
  • RIDOT's own design manual says to ask. "Crash data must be requested from the RIDOT Office of Safety". It also asks for 5 years of data and the KABCO injury levels.
  • Providence has a volunteer crash map built from police records. It's not official and has no licence, so we didn't use it.

What you need to do

  • File a public-records request on RIDOT's portal (dot.ri.gov → Business → Public Records) for 2021-2025 plus 2026: crashes, vehicles and people, the field list, and GPS points, as CSV.
  • In the same request, ask whether a paid tool may use the data. The law only forbids using it in damage lawsuits.
  • Send us the file you get. We already have the column map ready.

What we didn't check

  • How many crashes have GPS points (we have no file yet).
  • Rhode Island's crash costs and rates: its manuals don't list them.
  • Whether RIDOT plans an online portal.
