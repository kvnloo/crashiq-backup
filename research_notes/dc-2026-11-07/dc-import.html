Washington DC: crash data for CrashIQ

Ready, with one catch  Checked 7 Nov 2026

1. The data

DC's transport department (DDOT) publishes every police crash report online, updated every night: 356,985 crashes since 2008, plus a table of every person in them (906,895 rows). No login needed.

2. The catch: injury levels

Deaths and serious injuries are exact. But DC lumps the two lighter levels (B "visible injury" and C "complained of pain") into one "minor" group. CrashIQ can't split them.

2025: 22 fatal crashes, 284 serious-injury crashes, 4,304 "minor", 13,565 no injury.

The data also has no crash type (rear-end, broadside…), no lighting, no weather and no road surface. Those tables would stay empty for DC.

3. Can CrashIQ place crashes from the street names?

We hid the GPS on 2,000 crashes from 2025 and let CrashIQ place them from the address alone.

Placed from the words88.6%
Within 250 ft of police GPS98.7%
Placed automatically today2.8% (only street-pair crashes; house addresses wait for your OK)

The 98.7% sounds good, but it doesn't prove much. DC police get their "GPS" from the address they type, so we're matching the same address twice.

Example: of 1,709 house-address crashes, 1,548 (9 in 10) sit within 5 ft of the DC address-list point for that house number.

It doesn't matter much in DC: every crash already has a point, so placement adds 0 crashes.

Two fixes for the shared street-name code: DC's "NW / NE / SW / SE" at the end of a name, and streets called just "N", "E", "S" or "W".

4. DC's own rules (manuals)

  • No published average crash rates by road type. California has these (TASAS); DC doesn't. You can only compare locations inside DC.
  • DC ranks hot spots by a mix: 25% how many crashes, 25% the crash rate, 50% how bad they were. It also uses HSM safety models at intersections.
  • Intersection rate is crashes per million entering vehicles, the same as California.
  • Crash costs: DC uses FHWA's national costs (2024 dollars: fatal $15,988,000, serious $1,705,100, minor $384,000, possible $204,600, no injury $18,100).
  • DC uses 3 years of crashes, not California's 5.
  • Developers' traffic studies haven't needed a crash analysis since 2022.

Full details with page numbers: research_notes/dc-2026-11-07/dc-methods.md.

5. What's built

A DC reader that downloads the crashes and people and turns them into CrashIQ's crash format, with 7 offline tests on 95 real crashes. It isn't connected to the app yet.

What we didn't check

  • Crashes on federal land (Park Police: Rock Creek Pkwy, Suitland Pkwy, parts of the Mall) aren't in this data. We didn't count how many are missing.
  • How many crashes DDOT leaves out because it couldn't map them.
  • DC's national address-database coverage (the server refused our query). We used DC's own address list instead.
  • A comparison with the federal fatal-crash file (FARS).
  • DC's current safety plan PDF and its design manual's safety chapter.
