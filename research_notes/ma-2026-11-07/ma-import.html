Massachusetts: can CrashIQ use it?

Yes, mostly. Statewide crash records are free to download, with GPS on about 97% of crashes and KABCO severity. Two catches: the licence for 2020 and later, and Boston.

Checked live on Nov 7, 2026. Group: A* (open, one catch to clear).

1. The state data (MassDOT "IMPACT")

About 131,000 crashes a year (2025: 130,896), every year from 2002 to last night (newest crash: Nov 6, 2026).
Example: crash 5582854, Oct 8, 2025, 3 PM, Lowell, "FARWELL ST / CALVARY ST", no injury, with its GPS point and the people in it.
GPS on 95–97% of crashes every year (2025: 97.3%).
Severity is KABCO, per crash and per person, and people link to their crash by crash number. Old reports (before 2019) use older words; the importer translates them.
Example: "Non-fatal injury - Incapacitating" (2018) = A.
Each crash also carries the road's traffic volume (AADT), road class, lanes and speed limit. California's data doesn't.

2. Licence

2002–2019: public domain. MassDOT: "can be used by anyone for any purpose."
2020–2026: not stated. Only a disclaimer, which also says the recent years "should not be used for analysis". Nothing forbids reuse, but nothing grants it. We should ask MassDOT in writing.

4. Our own placement test

2,000 random 2025 crashes in Middlesex County. We hid the GPS and let CrashIQ place each crash from the street names, the way it does in California.

98.5% already have good GPS. Only 30 of 2,000 don't, and 27 of those have no street names either. Our locator would add 1 crash (0.05%).
Street pairs: 73% placed automatically, 96% within 250 ft of the state's point.
Example: "FARWELL ST / CALVARY ST" lands at that corner.
House addresses (41% of crashes): 96% found on the state's address list, 83% within 250 ft. Rural addresses miss more, because the address point sits on the house, not the road.
The fairest test is crashes where the officer clicked the spot on a map: 85% within 250 ft. That's below the 98% bar we set in California, so in Massachusetts the locator should check the state's GPS, not replace it.
What breaks: ramp names ("RAMP-RT 495 SB TO WEST MAIN ST EB"), both streets in one field ("Washington St Rte W / Lesher St Rte S", fixed in the importer), and the same street name in many towns ("MAIN ST & SCHOOL ST"), fixed by picking the crash's town first.

5. Importer

Built and tested (6 tests, offline). It reads any year and county, links the people, and turns each crash into CrashIQ's California shape. Not switched on in the app yet.

6. Massachusetts rules (methods)

Crash rates: per million entering vehicles (intersections) and per million vehicle miles (roads). Statewide average: 0.78 at signals, 0.57 at stop signs. Use 5 years of data (3 at least).
Crash costs (2024): K $19,435,000 · A $1,112,900 · B $354,100 · C $208,000 · O $20,900. California combines K and A; Massachusetts doesn't.
Ranking: any injury crash counts 21, a damage-only crash counts 1 ("EPDO"). The worst 5% of crash clusters in each region qualify for federal safety money, and each one needs a Road Safety Audit.
Example: in the Boston region (MAPC) an intersection qualifies at 108 points or more.
Different from California: Massachusetts prefers prediction models with "Empirical Bayes" (a weighted average of observed and predicted crashes); Caltrans avoids it. No critical-rate formula was found.

What you need to do

1. Email MassDOT Traffic & Safety (TrafficSafetyAnalysis@dot.state.ma.us) asking for written OK to use the 2020+ crash data in a paid product, with credit and the federal "not evidence" notice.
2. Decide on Boston: wait for Boston police data (a records request), or ship with a warning that Boston is under-counted.
3. Request MassDOT's free Safety Alternatives Analysis spreadsheets (Methods 1 and 2) on the HSIP page. They contain the state's preferred list of countermeasure factors.

What we didn't check

MassDOT's answer on the licence; 2015–2017 statistics (the server refused); the state's mile-marker map for freeway crashes; OpenStreetMap (we used Census streets only); Boston police data access; Worcester and Springfield beyond a search; the crash-cost method PDF (the site blocked the download).
