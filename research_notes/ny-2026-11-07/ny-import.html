New York: what crash data CrashIQ can use

Hermes, 7 Nov 2026 · branch state/ny · every number checked live today

Verdict

New York City works like California. The rest of the state only partly. NYC publishes every crash with a map point and street names, free for any use. The state publishes crashes too, but without a map point or street names; we can place the state-highway ones (about 4 in 10 outside NYC) by their mile-post sign. Full detail for every road (KABCO injury levels, exact spot) is in NYSDOT's CLEAR system, which only government staff and their consultants can log into.

1. The sources

NYC crash records — 85,559 crashes in 2025, 97% with a map point, 9 in 10 with two street names or a house address. Free, no limits on use ("There are no restrictions on the use of Open Data").
Example: "WEST 170 STREET at OGDEN AVENUE, Bronx, 06:13". People in the crash link to it by one ID. Injury is only "injured" or "killed", not the K-A-B-C levels.
State DMV crash table — about 381,000 crashes a year, 2021–2024 (2025 not out yet). Free for any use. No map point, no street names, no case number.
Example: a 2024 crash says only "ERIE County, WEST SENECA, property damage, marker 219 53121268". Without a marker it says just the town.
CLEAR (NYSDOT) — every crash since 2012, on every road, with a map point, street names and K-A-B-C. Login for government staff and their consultants; everyone else asks by FOIL.
We found two real CLEAR-style exports posted online (a Town of Gardiner study and a state utility filing) and built the reader from their columns: case number, coordinates, severity, serious injuries, on-street and cross-street.
Other big cities — Buffalo posts 911 crash calls (10,174 in 2025, 92% with a point), not police crash reports. Rochester has a dashboard only. Syracuse and Albany: nothing found.

2. How well CrashIQ places them

98%
of 500 state-highway crashes matched a NYSDOT mile-post sign, and 99.6% of those land inside the crash's own county.
Example: marker "9 84041008" becomes the point of the sign " 9 84041008" on Route 9. Signs are every 0.1 mile, so the crash is within about 260 ft along the road; which side and which lane is unknown.
57%
of 2,000 NYC crashes placed automatically from the street names alone (GPS hidden), and 99.2% of those were within 250 ft of the real point (at least 98.5% with 95% confidence). Another 20% placed from house addresses, 98.7% within 250 ft.
What didn't place: crashes that name only a highway ("BELT PARKWAY", 20%), and pairs of streets that cross twice. We also had to turn "E 26 ST" into "E 26th St" first; that fix belongs in the shared name code.
What it adds: NYC crashes with no GPS: in 2024 we place 2,263 of 7,619 (1,524 automatically); in 2025, 1,232 of 2,641. That is 1.4–2.5% more of all NYC crashes on the map.
Honest catch: NYC's map points for these crashes were probably made from the same street names, so "99% within 250 ft" shows we agree with the city's geocoder, not with an officer's GPS.

3. New York's own rules (manuals)

NYSDOT's Red Book and Yellow Book (both April 2026) and Design Manual chapter 5 (April 2026). Full notes with page numbers.
  • Hot spots are found by fatal + serious-injury crashes above what the NY model predicts for that kind of road (Empirical Bayes), over 5 years — not by comparing a crash rate with a base rate like TASAS.
  • Crash costs (2024 dollars): K $18,186,968 · A $991,313 · B $309,976 · C $194,893 · O $18,794.
  • Study period: 3 years normally, 2 if AADT over 100,000, 5 if under 2,000.
  • Average crash rates: the latest public table is 2017–2019 (e.g. urban 4-lane divided road with full access control 1.33 crashes per million vehicle-miles; urban 4-leg signal 0.56 per million entering vehicles). Newer rates are only inside CLEAR.

4. What we built

Three readers, not yet switched on in the app: NYC crashes + people; the state DMV table with the mile-post join; and a CLEAR export reader that takes a spreadsheet or shapefile table and turns its coordinates into map points. 18 offline tests pass.

What you need to do

  1. Get one CLEAR export (crash list and person list, one county, one year) from a NYSDOT or county contact, or by FOIL, so we can check the reader against today's CLEAR columns.
  2. Ask NYSDOT (dot.sm.clear@dot.ny.gov) whether consultants may load CLEAR exports into a paid tool.
  3. Decide what CrashIQ shows for state crashes that only name a town (about 58% outside NYC).

What we didn't check

  • The national address database (NAD) for New York: its server timed out today.
  • Whether NYSDOT's mile-post layer and the state address points may be used in a paid product (no licence text either way).
  • Mile-post accuracy against officers' own GPS (no public source has both).
  • OpenStreetMap streets for NYC (we used Census TIGER only); NYC vehicle table; current NY crash rates.
