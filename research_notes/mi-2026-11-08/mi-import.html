Michigan: the data is there, the permission isn't

Michigan's crash website (Traffic Crash Facts) shows every crash with its GPS point and injury level, free and without a login. But its rules say non-commercial use only, and they warn that scraping gets you blocked. Bulk data has to be requested from the State Police. So no Michigan pack yet.

What we found

  • All of Michigan, 2004-2025. 288,880 crashes in 2024 alone, with GPS points and real injury levels (fatal, serious, minor, possible, none).
  • The catch is the terms. They read: "noncommercial and non-profit use … Bulk downloads should be requested". CrashIQ is paid, so we didn't build a reader that pages through it.
  • Southeast Michigan has an open map layer. SEMCOG (the Detroit-area planning agency) publishes 2016-2025 crashes for 7 counties, with streets and injury counts. Example: 129,853 crashes in 2024, 45% of the state. But it's regional, and its licence doesn't cover the police's own records.
  • The police tool (Mi-CAT) is for police and approved safety partners only.

What you need to do

  • Email the State Police crash unit (CrashTCRS@michigan.gov). Ask for a statewide crash extract for 2021-2025, its field list, and whether a paid tool may use it.
  • Or say if a Detroit-area-only pack from SEMCOG is worth doing while you wait.

What we didn't check

  • How well crashes place (no open statewide sample we may use).
  • Michigan's crash costs and rates.
  • Detroit's city crash feed.
