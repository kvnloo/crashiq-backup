Louisiana: crash data for CrashIQ

Hermes, 8 Nov 2026. Checked live today. Full notes: la-import.md, column map: la-column-map.json.

Short answer: Louisiana is locked. DOTD's crash records need an access grant from its Highway Safety Section. We built no pack. A copy of DOTD's file shows its format places well: 86% of placed crashes land within 250 ft of DOTD's own point.

1. What Louisiana has

  • DOTD crash database (run by LSU CARTS): every crash in the state, with DOTD's map point, the street, the cross street and the distance. You ask DOTDCrashData@LA.gov for access.
  • New Orleans: the city's safety dashboard layer is open, 100,493 crashes 2019-2024 with K/A/B/C/O, but no street names.Example: 2024 has 17,285 crashes.
  • Baton Rouge: open, public domain, about 15,000 crashes a year since 2023, but only "injury yes/no", not K/A/B/C.
  • Two consultants posted copies of DOTD's file online. We used one only to test the format; it isn't ours to use.

2. Would CrashIQ place the crashes?

  • On 2,000 New Orleans crashes from 2019, we hid DOTD's point and placed them from the street names: 76% placed.
  • Within 250 ft of DOTD's point: 86%.Misses are mostly freeway ramps and the I-10 service road ("I-10 SRVRD").
  • Watch the distance unit: 18% of distances are in miles, not feet.

3. What you need to do

  1. Email DOTDCrashData@LA.gov: ask for Louisiana Crash Tool access, or a statewide 2020-2024 crash, vehicle and person file with coordinates.
  2. Ask them in writing if the data may be used inside a paid tool.
  3. Send us one file (one parish, one year) with its data dictionary, and we build the reader.

4. What we didn't check

  • How many Baton Rouge crashes have coordinates.
  • Lafayette, Shreveport, Jefferson Parish: no open crash tables found.
  • DOTD's public-records route instead of the Safety Section.
