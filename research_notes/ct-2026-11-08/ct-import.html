Connecticut: every crash in the state, on the map, 2015–2026

In one line: UConn's Connecticut Crash Data Repository publishes every crash since 2015 (about 100,000 a year) with a map point, the worst injury (K A B C O), the road and its people and vehicles. No login, no sign-up. It has no licence that says a paid tool may use it.

1. What Connecticut has

WhatExample
Map point100% of Hartford's 6,107 crashes in 2024.
Injury levelPer crash and per person: Suspected Serious Injury (A) = A.
RoadEvery crash. State roads come as 84-W or 187-N; CrashIQ reads them as I 84 and CT 187. A cross street on 1 crash in 4.
People, vehiclesLinked by crash number: pedestrians, cyclists, motorcycles, heavy trucks all found.
MissingVehicle movements (so no collision diagrams), primary collision factor.

2. Does it place? Yes, by its own point

98%
When we hid the point and placed Hartford crashes from the two road names alone, 98% landed within 250 ft of the real spot (84% of crashes that name a cross street placed). Since every crash already has a point, this is a cross-check, not a gap-filler.

3. Live test: Farmington Avenue, Hartford (1.19 miles, 2021–2025)

498 crashes
2 fatal, 6 serious (A), 76 minor (B), 65 possible (C), 349 property damage. Crash cost $32.7 million at CTDOT's own costs. 10 findings. PDF and Excel built (74 KB, 39 KB).

4. How Connecticut does safety studies

  • Crash costs: CTDOT's safety plan (FY2027, Table A-2): fatal $6,415,389, serious $338,576, minor $123,646, possible $69,541, property damage $11,186. CrashIQ prints them, cited.
  • No crash-rate tables: CTDOT compares with a statewide average inside its own software but doesn't publish it.
  • CrashIQ uses the national method (Highway Safety Manual critical rate) for now.

What you need to do

  1. Ask CTDOT / UConn in writing whether a paid tool may load the repository's public crash layers, and what notice to show. (Their words: "for research and informational purposes".)
  2. OK the CTDOT crash costs as printed (the plan gives no dollar year).

What we didn't check

  • Towns other than Hartford for placement.
  • The contributing-circumstance codes (not mapped yet).
  • data.ct.gov's copy of the same data.
