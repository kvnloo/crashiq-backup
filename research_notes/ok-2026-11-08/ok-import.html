Oklahoma: crash data for CrashIQ

Hermes, 8 Nov 2026. Checked live today. Full notes: ok-import.md, column map: ok-column-map.json.

Short answer: Oklahoma is locked. Every crash is in ODOT's SAFE-T, which needs a personal account and says the data "may not be used for private projects". What's open statewide is only fatal and injury crashes from 2019-2021. We built no pack.

1. What Oklahoma has

  • ODOT SAFE-T: every crash since 1998, mapped, K/A/B/C/O, exports to Excel. Account needed; private-sector users sign a permit.
  • OCERS: the new police crash system (DPS). Login only.
  • Open, but partial: OHSO's maps of fatal, serious and minor-injury crashes, 2019-2021 (about 9,000 a year).Example: Tulsa County 2021 has 2,093, and none of its fender-benders.
  • Also open: ODOT's I-35 layer (I-35 only), a southern-Oklahoma region 2023-2025, Oklahoma City 2017-2019, Tulsa region 2011-2020.

2. Would CrashIQ place the crashes?

  • On 2,000 Tulsa County crashes from 2021, we hid the GPS and placed them from the street names: 49% placed.
  • Within 250 ft of the true spot: 63%.Officers write the same street many ways ("71 ST. S.", "E. 71ST STREET"), and the file gives no distance from the intersection.
  • OHSO's copy has GPS on every crash; names matter only where an export lacks it.

  3. Send us one SAFE-T export (one county, one year, all fields) and its data dictionary, and we build the reader.

4. What we didn't check
