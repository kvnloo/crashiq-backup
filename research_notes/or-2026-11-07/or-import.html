Oregon: can CrashIQ use it?

Yes, the data is open and very good. Every Oregon crash (city streets, county roads, state highways), 2015-2024, downloads with no login. 98.8% already have a map point, injuries are KABCO for every person, and the crash, vehicle and person tables link. The catch: Oregon gives no licence for a paid product. We need ODOT's written OK.

1. Where the data is

  • ODOT's yearly crash database (one 20 MB file per year), from the Crash Data Products page. 48,414 crashes in 2024.
    Example: crash 2058819, 20 Dec 2024, 8 pm, NE Broadway St at NE 15th Ave, Portland: a pedestrian killed, hit and run. Every person in it has a K/A/B/C/O injury level.
  • ODOT's online crash map layer, 2019-2024, one row per crash, with ODOT's own K/A/B/C/O.
    We checked: the person-by-person severity from the yearly file matches ODOT's crash severity on all 8,874 Multnomah County crashes of 2024.
  • Cities (Portland, Salem, Eugene, Bend) don't publish their own crash lists. Portland's transportation bureau says ODOT keeps the official record. So Portland = ODOT's data: 7,182 crashes in 2024, 98% on the map.
  • Address points: Oregon has a free statewide list (1.9 million addresses, public domain). Not needed: Oregon crash reports never give a house number.

2. How many crashes CrashIQ itself can place (Portland test)

2,000 random Multnomah County crashes from 2024. We hid ODOT's map point and let CrashIQ place each crash from its two street names, on the Census street map.

  • City streets work.
    A 2024 fatal crash at "SE Woodstock Blvd" and "SE 82nd Ave" is placed on that corner, under 1 ft from ODOT's point.
  • Accuracy is a bit below our California bar (98% needed to auto-place); 96.6% here. Not yet explained.
  • Freeways don't work from names: ODOT writes its own short highway and ramp names.
    A 2024 I-84 crash reads "BANFIELD FY" at "WB EF 181ST AVE C3": a westbound ramp code at 181st Ave, not a street on any map.
    But every highway crash also has a highway number and milepoint, like Caltrans postmiles. That would be the fix.
  • Crashes with no map point: names add nothing. Only 156 in the county all year (1.8%), and 151 of them name just one road.

3. What we built

A reader for both ODOT products, with tests that run without internet on 40 real Portland crashes. It is not switched on in the app yet (that waits for the state-pack design).

4. Oregon's own rules (manuals)

  • Rates: intersections per million entering vehicles, with a statewide table; an intersection over the "90th percentile" rate is flagged.
    Urban 4-way signal: average 0.477, flag above 0.860 crashes per million entering vehicles.
    State-highway segments per million vehicle-miles (Table II).
    Urban interstate 2024: 0.54.
  • Crash costs: fatal and serious injury are priced together, and so are minor and possible injury.
    Urban city street: fatal or serious crash $1,110,000; minor injury crash $81,300; damage only $21,800 (2019 values).
  • Screening: ODOT's own SPIS list (0.1-mile pieces, 3 years) plus the usual critical-rate test.
  • Full details, page numbers and the differences from California: or-methods.md.

What you need to do

  1. Email ODOT's crash unit (ODOTTDSCrashRequestGroup@odot.oregon.gov) and ask for written permission to use Oregon crash data inside a paid tool. Their disclaimer expects consultants to re-release the data, but doesn't say "commercial use is OK".
  2. Pick one: the yearly files (every person, 2015-2024) or the online layer (crash only, 2019-2024). We suggest the yearly files.

What we didn't check

  • Any county other than Multnomah; the app's combined OSM + Census map (we used the Census map only).
  • Why accuracy is 96.6% and not 98%.
  • How many freeway crashes a milepoint method would place (ODOT's milepoint layer wasn't tested).
  • The national address list (NAD) for Oregon (its server refused the query).
  • Which cost table ODOT's current benefit-cost workbook uses (2012 or 2019 values).
