Virginia: crash data for CrashIQ

Hermes, 7 Nov 2026. Measured live today. Full notes: va-import.md, methods: va-methods.md (link).

Short answer: Virginia is the easiest state so far on the data. VDOT puts every reported crash online, free, no login, with a map point on almost all of them and KABCO severity. The only question is the licence for a paid tool.

1. What Virginia has

  • VDOT "Full Crash": 1,175,583 crashes, 2017 to August 2026. Example: 2024 has 129,244 crashes — exactly the number in DMV's official 2024 Crash Facts, so nothing is missing.
  • Map point on 99.995%. Only 61 crashes in ten years have no point. Example: one of them only says "RT 7".
  • KABCO severity on every crash, plus how many K, A, B and C people. Example: a crash with 1 A person and 2 C people is an A crash; VDOT's own label matched our count on all 17,527 crashes we checked.
  • Only one street box. The officer writes the location in one line, no separate cross street. Example: "FAIRFAX COUNTY PKWY NB / NOMES CT FRFX", or a house address "4300 COMMERCE ROAD".
  • People and vehicles: only the first driver, passenger and pedestrian of each crash are public.
  • House addresses: Virginia's statewide address points (3.9 million) are free under CC0, so fine for paid use.

3. Our street-name placement, tested

We hid the map point on 2,000 crashes in Richmond and 2,000 in Fairfax County (2024) and let CrashIQ find them from the street names alone.

RichmondFairfax
Had two street names63%45%

Within 250 ft of the real point97.5%95.9%

Close to California's 98% bar but under it. Most misses are ramps: "I-95 RAMP & COMMERCE RD" was put 1.7 miles off (only as a suggestion, not auto-placed). Virginia spellings the code doesn't know yet: "TPIKE", "HWAY", "PKWAY", "US-60E".

House addresses on the state's address points: 81% found, 64% within 250 ft, 79% within 400 ft (Richmond). Many say "3700 BLK", a block, not a house.

What it adds for Virginia: almost nothing — at most 6 crashes in ten years lack a point. VDOT already did the placing.

4. Virginia's own rules (manuals)

  • Crash costs 2026 (VDOT memo, Sept 2026): K $17,009,543 · A $998,777 · B $329,990 · C $190,773 · O $17,344. California's LRSM prices K and A together at $2.4–3.8M, so Virginia's fatal crash costs 4.5–7× more in a study.
  • Benefit/cost form: 3% discount rate, 5 years of crashes; B/C of 1 or more to score.
  • Ranking: VDOT ranks by "Potential for Safety Improvement" from its own prediction models, and lists the top 100.
  • Statewide average rates: not published as a table (only inside VDOT's Power BI tool). A VDOT study quotes 111.5 crashes per 100 million vehicle-miles for rural primary roads, 2017–2024.
  • Calibration factors for intersections (VTRC, March 2026). Example: urban 4-leg signal 1.59.
  • Details with page numbers: va-methods.md.

5. What we built

A Virginia reader that downloads VDOT's crashes and turns them into CrashIQ's crash format, with 7 offline tests. It is not switched on in the app yet (that waits for the state-pack design).

What you need to do

  • Decide: in Virginia studies, use Virginia's 2026 crash costs instead of Caltrans'.

What we didn't check
