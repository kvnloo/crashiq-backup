New Mexico: Albuquerque is open, the rest is by request

In one line: NMDOT only gives crash data on request, but the City of Albuquerque posts every crash in Bernalillo County, 2020–2024, with a map point, street names and K/A/B/C counts. CrashIQ reads it; it's kept off the menu until the reuse terms are clear.

1. What New Mexico has

WhatExample
StatewideBy request only. UNM: "will not release traffic crash data directly to end users."
Bernalillo County75,460 crashes, 2020–2024 (2024: 15,529).
Map point99.8% of crashes.
Street namesStreet + cross street or landmark: GIBSON BLVD SE, 100 ft E of CARLISLE BLVD SE.
Injury levelPeople killed and class A, B, C injured on each crash, so K A B C O.
MissingPeople and vehicle records, road surface, crash costs NMDOT signs off on.

2. Does it place without GPS? Yes, mostly

92%
of the Albuquerque crashes CrashIQ auto-placed from the street names alone (GPS hidden, 2,000 crashes from 2024) landed within 250 ft of UNM's point. Only 11 of 15,528 crashes lack a point, so this is a cross-check.

3. A real corridor

Central Avenue, San Mateo to Louisiana, 1.06 mi, 2020–2024: 779 crashes: 11 fatal, 18 serious, 65 minor, 165 possible, 520 property damage. 19 findings. No dollar total yet. PDF and Excel exports work.

What you need to do

  1. Ask the City of Albuquerque and NMDOT in writing whether a paid tool may use the Bernalillo crash layer, and what notice to show.
  2. For other counties, send NMDOT's crash data request form (dot.nm.gov/traffic-records-system) for the counties and years you need.
  3. Ask NMDOT's HSIP manager for its crash cost per injury level.
  4. Decide whether to offer New Mexico as "Albuquerque only".

What we didn't check

A real NMDOT request export, the 2016–2019 Albuquerque layers, house addresses, NMDOT's network-screening memos.
