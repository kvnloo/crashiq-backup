Utah: crash data for CrashIQ

Hermes, 8 Nov 2026. Status: ready to use (active), with one catch.

What Utah has

  • UDOT publishes every reported crash since 2019 on a public map service, no login, updated every night. About 50,000 to 65,000 crashes a year.
  • Every crash has a GPS point. Example: crash 825937130 at 6844 S Highland Dr sits at 40.6268, -111.8340.
  • Severity is the standard K/A/B/C/O scale, with the number of people at each level.
  • Pedestrian, bicycle, motorcycle and truck crashes are flagged yes/no. There are no driver or vehicle records, so road-user movements and collision diagrams are empty.

Catch: the licence

UDOT says the data is "for informational purposes only and must be field verified prior to being used on a project" and is protected under 23 USC 407. It neither grants nor forbids paid use, so the licence is unclear.

Test run: State Street, South Salt Lake (2100 S to 3300 S, 1.5 mi, 2020-2024)

  • 396 crashes counted: K 1, A 13, B 57, C 75, no-injury 250.
  • Crash cost $95,123,826 at UDOT's 2026 costs. UDOT says to price fatal and serious-injury crashes at one blended cost ($3.9M) when working from crash history, so that's what CrashIQ does.
  • 187 crashes on I-80 where it crosses State Street were left out as freeway crashes, as they should be.
  • 9 findings; PDF and Excel exports both work (75 KB and 40 KB).

Street names

About a third of crashes name a cross street ("I-15, 249 ft south of 700 South") and two thirds give a house address. GPS does the placing, so this only matters for checking.

What you need to do

  1. Email UDOT Traffic & Safety (Chris Whipple, chriswhipple@utah.gov) for written permission to use the crash layer in CrashIQ.

What we didn't check

  • Address points for the house-address crashes.
  • The 2015-2019 state open-data set.
  • Statewide average crash rates (UDOT doesn't publish them by road type) and Utah traffic counts.
