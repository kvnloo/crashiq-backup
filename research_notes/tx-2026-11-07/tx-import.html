Texas: crash data for CrashIQ

Hermes, 7 Nov 2026. Bucket B: free sign-up, then download.

The short answer

Texas has one statewide crash file, TxDOT's CRIS public extract. It's free, but you sign up first. Every crash comes with its people and vehicles, the injury level on the usual K-A-B-C-O scale, and two locations: what the officer wrote and where TxDOT's own map put it. The reader for it is built and tested on a practice file made from TxDOT's published layout. It still needs your download to run on real crashes.

How much is on the map

  • 92.5% of Dallas County's 47,628 crashes in 2025 already have a map point. On public roads it's 98.8%. Almost every crash without a point happened in a parking lot (3,007 of 3,562).
  • Austin's open copy shows 100%, but Austin publishes only the crashes it could map, so that isn't the real rate.
  • Houston and Fort Worth publish no crash list. San Antonio has injury crashes 2021–2025 with one street name each. Dallas city has 2019 only, marked "not for commercial uses".

Can CrashIQ find crashes from the street names?

We tested 2,000 Dallas County crashes. We hid each crash's map point and gave CrashIQ only the two street names, for example E BELT LINE RD & S HIGHLAND DR.

City streets work almost as well as in California. Highways don't yet: this copy has no "500 ft north of" distance, and CrashIQ can't read Texas highway names like IH0035E or SL0012 until the shared code learns them. It also treats I-35E and I-35W, two different freeways in Dallas, as the same road. Texas mile markers would fix highways, but that isn't built yet. Few crashes without a point have both street names, so names add only 40 more crashes (0.08%).

Texas rules CrashIQ must follow

  • Crash rate: crashes per 100 million vehicle miles for road sections, per million entering vehicles for intersections. Compare with TxDOT's statewide average, e.g. an urban state highway averages 215.41 and a rural one 94.00 (2024).
  • Crash cost (2026 safety funding): $4,290,000 per fatal or serious-injury crash and $360,000 per minor-injury crash. Possible-injury and damage-only crashes get no cost. California prices all five levels.
  • Years: 5 years of crashes (at least 3; 8 for walking, biking or truck studies). Safety funding uses exactly the last 3.
  • Benefit vs cost: TxDOT's "Safety Improvement Index" (≥ 1.0 qualifies), with its own percent reduction for each fix, e.g. a new signal 20%, a median barrier 50%.
  • No statewide intersection average rate and no "critical rate" formula found. Details with page numbers: tx-methods.md.

What you need to do

  1. Sign up at cris.txdot.gov and pick "Download interface data from Crash Reports for statistical purposes". Log in at least once a month, or TxDOT deletes the account after 30 days.
  2. Request the Public Extract as CSV for county DALLAS, 1 Jan–31 Dec 2025. Then do the same for 2024, 2023, 2022 and 2021. Each request can cover only one year.
  3. If you have time, also request Travis 2025 and Harris 2025.
  4. Choose a file password. When the email comes, download the zip, open it with 7-Zip or Keka, and put every file in ~/.hermes/cache/scratch/states/tx/cris/dallas_2025/ (one folder per county and year).
  5. Email TRF_CRASH@txdot.gov to ask whether CrashIQ may show these crashes inside a paid product.

What we didn't check

  • The statewide share of crashes with a map point (needs your download).
  • House addresses: the national address list wasn't responding today.
  • Whether paid use is allowed: TxDOT gives no licence.
  • Which yearly traffic volumes city streets use, and Texas's other tables (critical rates, intersection averages).
