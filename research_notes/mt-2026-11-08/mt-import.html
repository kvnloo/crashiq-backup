Montana: every crash in the state, on the map, 2016–2025

In one line: MDT publishes all 219,418 crashes from 2016 to 2025 with a map point and the crash's worst injury (K A B C O). No login. It has no street names, no time of day and no people, and no licence that says a paid tool may use it.

1. What Montana has

WhatExample
Map point100% (all but 2 junk rows). MDT puts each crash at its route reference point, so crashes at one intersection share one dot.
Injury levelPer crash: Suspected Serious Injury = A. 4% say Unknown: counted as "injury, level not reported".
Crash detailsCollision type (Wild Animal is 14%), weather, road surface, light, city, county.
MissingStreet names, time of day, report number, drivers, vehicles, people killed/injured counts.

3. Live test: Grand Avenue, Billings (1.0 mile, 2020–2024)

131 crashes
1 fatal, 1 serious (A), 11 minor (B), 44 possible (C), 13 unknown-level, 61 property damage. 4 findings. PDF and Excel both built (42 KB, 36 KB).

4. How Montana does safety studies

  • Crash costs: a software vendor's help page lists MDT's defaults (fatal $6,168,300, serious $326,400, minor $119,200, possible $67,100, property damage $10,800), with no year. CrashIQ doesn't print them until MDT confirms.
  • No crash-rate tables were found. MDT screens with its own models; its safety manual is internal.
  • CrashIQ uses the national method (Highway Safety Manual critical rate) for now.

What you need to do

  1. Ask MDT in writing whether a paid tool may load its public crash layer, and what notice it must show. (MDT's text: "provided as-is … for planning purposes only".)
  2. Ask MDT for its crash costs per injury level and their dollar year.
  3. Decide: should MDT's "Unknown" severity count as injured (now: "level not reported")?

What we didn't check

  • How close MDT's reference-point dot is to where the crash really happened.
  • Adding street names by joining MDT's route layer (possible, not built).
  • Fuller crash records MDT gives on a public-information request.
