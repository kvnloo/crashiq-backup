North Carolina: can CrashIQ use it?

Checked live 2026-11-07. Short answer: not the whole state yet. The state's full crash system is for agencies only. Four open pieces work today.

1. The whole state: locked

NCDOT keeps every crash since 1990 in a program called TEAAS. Only government staff, towns, police, planning agencies and researchers get it, after a training class.

Example: a consultant working for an MPO can get it through that MPO; a private company on its own cannot.

What NCDOT does publish for everyone: a count table of every crash (about 305,000 a year) with only county and city, no location.

Example: "2024, Durham, angle crash, no injury" with no street or point.

2. Open pieces that work today

  • Fatal and serious-injury crashes, whole state, 2016-2025: 53,917 crashes, with GPS and street names.
    Example: "NC 58, 0.1 mile north of SR 1607, serious injury, motorcycle".
  • Pedestrian and bicycle crashes, whole state, 2007-2025: 58,328 crashes, GPS, injury level for the walker or rider and the driver. No street names.
  • Raleigh, 2015-today, updated daily: about 22,000 crashes a year with GPS, both street names and the distance from the cross street.
    Example: "Oakwood Ave, 220 ft east of Tarboro St, serious injury".
  • Durham, 2016-2025: about 6,000-9,000 crashes a year, GPS, streets, full injury levels.

Not usable: Charlotte (550,000 crashes with GPS, but its licence forbids commercial use), Greensboro (stops at 2023, no street names), Cary (no GPS), Winston-Salem (nothing published).

3. Does our street-name method work there?

We hid the GPS on 2,000 Raleigh crashes from 2025 and let CrashIQ place them from the street names, on the Census street map of Wake County.

As writtenWith 2 small NC fixes
Placed automatically59%72%
Within 250 ft of the police point62%56%

For crashes the officer wrote "at" the intersection, 83% land within 250 ft. The misses are mostly crashes written as "200 ft from" a street, where the officer's own point often doesn't match his distance.

Example of an NC fix: Raleigh writes I-440 as just "440". CrashIQ can't find a street named "440" until we teach it.

4. NC's own rules (they differ from California)

  • Crash rates: one statewide table, crashes per 100 million vehicle miles, by road system. Statewide average 203 for 2021-2025.
    Example: urban US routes 284, rural interstates 78.
  • No published intersection rate per million entering vehicles.
  • Costs per crash (2025): fatal $13,565,000, A $788,000, B $263,000, C $153,000, property damage $15,100.
  • NC flags problem spots with "warrants", not rankings. Example: an urban intersection with 12+ angle/turn crashes making up 60% of its crashes, a quarter of them in the last 2 years.
  • Study periods: 5 years for city intersections, 10 years for rural ones.

Details with page numbers: nc-methods.md (research_notes/nc-2026-11-07/ in the PR).

5. What we built

Readers for Raleigh, Durham and the two statewide NCDOT layers, with tests. They are not switched on in the app yet.

What you need to do

  • If a client agency or MPO has TEAAS, ask them for one crash export (one county, one year). That lets us build "upload your NC export".
  • Ask Raleigh (Open Raleigh), Durham and NCDOT Traffic Safety, in one email each, whether a paid tool may show their crash data. None of them says yes or no in writing.
  • Decide: NC has an "injury unknown" level. Count it like property damage (NC's way) or like a C injury (our California rule)?

What we didn't check

  • What a TEAAS export looks like (no public spec).
  • How much of NC the national address file covers (its search kept failing).
  • Placement outside Raleigh, and on the statewide fatal/serious layer.
  • Whether Raleigh will fill in the injury and weather fields again (blank since October 2024).
