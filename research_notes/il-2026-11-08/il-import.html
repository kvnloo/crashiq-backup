Illinois: ready

IDOT puts every Illinois crash, 2014 to 2025, on its public map site, free and without a login: about 300,000 a year, Chicago included. Each has a GPS point and the full injury scale (fatal, A, B, C, no injury). CrashIQ now reads it. Last week's screen said Illinois needed a login; that was wrong.

What we found

  • GPS on 99.8% of crashes. 2025: 538 of 303,087 have no point.
  • Real injury levels. Example: Western Ave in Chicago, 2021-2025: 1 fatal, 13 A, 67 B, 49 C crashes.
  • Street names only for 2025. Older years give a route number, not a street. They're still placed by GPS.
  • Names place well in the suburbs. In DuPage County, hiding the GPS and placing from the two street names put 32% of crashes on the map, 92% of them within 250 ft. Chicago's police write a house number instead, which we didn't test.
  • One messy year. In 2019 a quarter of the rows have columns shifted by a stray comma. CrashIQ reads around it.

Test run

N Western Ave, Chicago (Fullerton to Belmont), 1.0 mi, 2021-2025: 796 crashes counted (1 fatal, 129 injury, 666 property damage). PDF and Excel built. No crash cost: IDOT prices people, not crashes.

What you need to do

  • Ask IDOT (DOT.DTS.DataRequests@illinois.gov) in writing whether CrashIQ may show these crash layers in a paid tool. 2014-2020 say CC BY-SA; 2021-2025 say nothing.
  • Optional: ask IDOT for street names for 2014-2024 and its current crash costs per crash.

What we didn't check

  • House-number placement in Chicago.
  • The 2025 people and vehicle tables (published, not read yet).
  • Whether IDOT minds automated queries.
  • Traffic counts for crash rates.
