Kansas: crash data for CrashIQ

Hermes, 8 Nov 2026. Measured live today. Full notes: ks-import.md, methods: ks-methods.md.

Short answer: Kansas works in CrashIQ now. KDOT puts its crashes online, free, no login, each with a map point and the K/A/B/C counts. Two catches: no street names, and no clear licence for a paid tool.

1. What Kansas has

  • KDOT "Crashes in Kansas": 607,257 crashes, 2016 to 29 Oct 2026. Example: 2024 has 55,685.
  • A map point on every one, but KDOT only publishes crashes it could locate. Example: Kansas reported 61,495 crashes in 2024; 91% are on the map.
  • Severity is real KABCO: killed, disabling, non-incapacitating, possible injury counts per crash.
  • No street names at all. Example: a crash says "Wichita, Sedgwick County, local route", plus its point.
  • No driver or vehicle tables, so no movement diagrams.

2. Test on a real street

  • N Rock Rd, Wichita, Central to 13th (1 mile), 2020–2024: 233 crashes counted: 1 fatal, 1 serious, 26 minor, 46 possible injury, 159 property damage.
  • PDF and Excel came out fine (48 KB and 36 KB).
  • No dollar costs: KDOT prices each hurt person, CrashIQ prices each crash. Example: KDOT counts $14.0M per person killed, $774k per disabling injury (2019 dollars).

3. What you need to do

  • Ask KDOT (Safety Data Section) for a written OK to use the crash layer in a paid product. Its website terms say no reuse "without explicit permission from KDOT".
  • Decide whether Kansas costs should be priced per person, as KDOT does.

What we didn't check

  • Freeways built on US routes (Kellogg in Wichita) aren't flagged as freeway; check those corridors by hand.
  • KDOT's 2024 Crash Facts pages (the site blocks scripts); the 2023 book was used.
  • Placement accuracy: there are no street names to test against.
