Nevada: crash data for CrashIQ

Hermes, 8 Nov 2026. Checked live today. Full notes: nv-import.md, column map: nv-column-map.json.

Short answer: Nevada is locked. NDOT hands out its crash data only on request, and its open map has been switched off. We built no pack. When you get a file from NDOT, its columns are already mapped, and its street format places like California's.

1. What Nevada has

  • NDOT crash data, 2020-2024, statewide: by request form only. Its open layer answers Service ... not started.
  • NDOT's terms give no permission for a paid tool; they only say the data is protected under 23 U.S.C. 407 and "all users shall comply with ... all applicable laws". Licence unclear.
  • Las Vegas city feed: 70,888 crashes, but only 2015-2020, and no map points. Example: "NARROW CANYON ST, 210 ft S of ALEXANDER RD, injury B".
  • Copies of NDOT data posted by consultants exist online. We did not use them: they are not NDOT's to give out.

2. Would CrashIQ place the crashes?

  • On 2,000 Las Vegas crashes from 2020, CrashIQ placed 66% from the two street names (73% of those that name a cross street).
  • Accuracy could not be measured: the feed has no GPS to check against.
  • What fails: freeways and ramps. Example: "US95S & LAS VEGAS BLVD TO US95S INT75". NDOT also writes routes run together, like IR15N for I-15.

3. What you need to do

  1. Send NDOT's Crash Data Request form for statewide, 2020-2024. Ask for map coordinates (X/Y) and written permission to use it in a paid tool.
  2. Ask NDOT when its crash map comes back, and under what licence.
  3. Send us one file (one county, one year) so we can confirm the column map.

4. What we didn't check

  • NDOT's HSIP manual (crash rates and costs).
  • Reno and Clark County open data beyond consultant copies.
