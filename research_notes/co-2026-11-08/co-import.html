Colorado: crash data for CrashIQ

Hermes, 8 Nov 2026. Status: ready to use (active), with two catches.

What Colorado has

  • CDOT posts one spreadsheet of every reported crash per year, 2007 to 2026, free, no login. About 95,000 to 103,000 crashes a year.
  • Most crashes have a GPS point: 76% in 2021, 98% in 2024, nearly all in 2025. Most also name the street and cross street, like "W 38TH AVE at KIPLING ST".
  • Severity is K/A/B/C/O, with the number of people at each level.
  • CrashIQ reads 2021 on. Older years use a different spreadsheet layout and aren't read yet.

Street names work the California way

On 2,000 Denver crashes from 2024, with the GPS hidden, CrashIQ placed 83% from the street names alone, and 89% of those landed within 250 ft of the real GPS point. So the crashes with no GPS (a quarter of 2021) can still be placed.

Catch 1: the download site

CDOT's files sit on a document system (OnBase). It let CrashIQ download three years this morning, then started showing a sign-in page to CrashIQ while a normal browser still got the file. If that happens, CrashIQ reports the year as missing rather than counting part of it.

Catch 2: crash costs are off for now

CDOT's 2025 costs (fatal $1.95M per person, serious injury $1.11M per person, no injury $18,000 per crash) are in its safety report to FHWA, but FHWA's site blocks automatic downloads, so we couldn't save and check the file. Costs stay off until it's saved.

Test run: E Colfax Ave, Denver (Colorado Blvd to Holly St, 1 mi, 2021-2025)

  • 290 crashes counted: K 4, A 11, B 38, C 32, no-injury 205.
  • 12 findings; PDF and Excel exports both work (75 KB and 41 KB).

Licence

CDOT says only that its crash listings "are protected under 23 U.S.C. Section 407". It neither grants nor forbids paid use, so the licence is unclear.

What you need to do

  1. Email CDOT (David Swenka, david.swenka@state.co.us, or Aaron Moss, aaron.moss@state.co.us) for written permission to use the crash listings in CrashIQ, and ask for a plain download link for the yearly files.
  2. Open the Colorado HSIP 2025 report on highways.dot.gov in your browser and save the PDF to ~/.hermes/state-manuals/co/, so the crash costs can be checked and turned on.

What we didn't check

  • Why the download site refuses CrashIQ.
  • 2007-2020.
  • CDOT's own safety curves (LOSS) and Colorado traffic counts.
