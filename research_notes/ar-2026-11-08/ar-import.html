Arkansas: crash data for CrashIQ

Hermes, 8 Nov 2026. Measured live today. Full notes: ar-import.md, methods: ar-methods.md.

Short answer: Arkansas works. The state DOT (ARDOT) puts every police crash report from 2021 to 2025 online, free, no login, with a map point on 99.6% of them and KABCO severity. A Little Rock corridor study ran end to end today. The catch is the licence: ARDOT gives none, and calls the data "not an official record".

1. What Arkansas has

  • 401,279 crashes, about 80,000 a year, 2021-2025. Only the newest five years are kept.
    Example: a December 2024 angle crash on N Rodney Parham Rd, Little Rock, "No Apparent Injury (O)", with its GPS point.
  • Map point on 99.6%. The 1,467 without one can't be placed: they have no road either.
  • Severity is KABCO per crash. People counts are only "killed" and "seriously injured", so injured totals are a floor.
  • No driver, vehicle or person records, and no weather.
  • Street names are squashed: the road is stored as NRODNEYPARHAMRD. CrashIQ can't read that yet, so it places every crash by its GPS point. A crash in a parking lot next to the corridor can't be told apart.

3. Arkansas's own methods

  • Crash costs from ARDOT's HSIP Process (Dec 2022), Appendix E: fatal $6.34M, serious $335k, minor $123k, possible $69k, property damage $11k.
  • Screening: ARDOT uses a critical fatal-and-serious crash rate. CrashIQ uses the national critical rate for now.
  • Statewide average rates: ARDOT computes them but doesn't publish them, so CrashIQ shows no rate comparison.

What you need to do

  • Decide whether to ask ARDOT's Traffic Safety Section for written permission to show this data in a paid tool.
  • Optional: ask them for the statewide average crash rates.

What we didn't check

  • Whether 2025 is complete (78,000 crashes against about 81,000 in other years).
  • How accurate ARDOT's map points are (nothing independent to compare with).
  • Whether Little Rock publishes its own crash feed.
