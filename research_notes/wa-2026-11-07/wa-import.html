Washington: Seattle is ready, the rest of the state needs WSDOT's OK

In one line: Seattle publishes every crash with a map point, the two street names and each person's injury level, under a licence that allows paid use. WSDOT also shows every crash in the state with a map point, but has no licence and no street names in the public version.

2. Can CrashIQ place crashes from street names? Yes

99.6%
of Seattle crashes CrashIQ placed from street names alone (GPS hidden) landed within 250 ft of the city's own point. 2,000 crashes from 2025; 83.6% placed automatically.
98.3%
Tacoma, using the WSDOT export format a user would upload: 94.9% placed automatically, 98.3% of those within 250 ft. Example: "686 ft southeast of Division Ave on S Stadium Way" was placed automatically, 222 ft from WSDOT's point.
One catch: Seattle writes block crashes as "A between B and C". Today's locator puts them on the corner (only 59% within 250 ft). Putting them in the middle of the block fixes it (99.7%). That needs a small change in the shared locator.

Other fixes the shared code needs: "M L KING JR WAY" must match "Martin Luther King Jr Way"; ramps and bridges ("SR99 TUNNEL SB"); state-highway crashes in the export give only a milepost, so they need WSDOT's route system.

3. Washington does safety studies differently

  • No crash rates. WSDOT "no longer uses crash rates for decision-making". There's no table of average rates like Caltrans TASAS.
  • Crash costs (2024 HSIP plan): fatal and serious both $4,308,200, minor $304,200, possible $180,600, property damage $19,300. California prices them by intersection type.
  • Screening uses the Highway Safety Manual: expected vs. typical fatal and serious crashes (calibration 1.00), not hot spots.
  • Five full years of data, and every page or table needs the federal §407 footer.
  • City grants need a Local Road Safety Plan and the report numbers of the fatal/serious crashes 2020–2024.

Details with page numbers: research_notes/wa-2026-11-07/wa-methods.md; data: wa-import.md, wa-summary.json.

4. Built

A Seattle reader and a WSDOT reader (for the export users bring and for the public portal), with tests on real 2025 rows. Not switched on in the app yet.

What you need to do

  1. Email WSDOT's crash data branch (crashanalysis@wsdot.wa.gov) and ask in writing: may a paid tool load the public portal records and the exports users request, and what notice must it show?
  2. Request one export to test with: King County, 2025, all roads, Excel (form 780-032).
  3. Decide: WSDOT marks some injuries "Unknown". Count them as injured-level-unknown (now) or not?

What we didn't check

  • Placing state-highway and county-road crashes by milepost.
  • House-number placement from WSDOT block numbers (address points exist statewide; accuracy not measured).
  • Whether Seattle's per-person table falls under its "list of individuals" rule for commercial use.
  • WSDOT's internal crash-cost sheet (may be newer) and its countermeasure short list.
