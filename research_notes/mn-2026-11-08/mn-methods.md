# Minnesota methods (cited)

1. **Crash costs**: MnDOT Table A.1, "Recommended standard values for use in cost-effectiveness & benefit-cost analysis
   in SFY 2026" (Office of Transportation System Management, August 2025; file dated 28 July 2025), p. 1, "Per-crash
   comprehensive costs", Most Likely column: K $16,500,000; A $1,700,000; B $380,000; C $180,000; O $18,000.
   Note 7 (p. 2): built from Minnesota's three-year crash history with FHWA's 2018 Crash Costs for Highway Safety
   Analysis. No dollar year is printed for crash costs (the CO2 line is 2024$); the pack labels them "SFY 2026".
   PDF in `~/.hermes/state-manuals/mn/` (sha256 386858f0…8fc5).
2. **Statewide average rates**: missing. MnDOT's crash-rate page gives only formulas (per MVM for segments, per MEV for
   intersections); the BCA guidance says facility-type averages come from District Traffic Engineering offices.
3. **Screening**: HSM critical rate against the corridor's own sites (national, user's decision). MnDOT's own critical
   crash rate method isn't built.
4. **Minimum years**: not found in the documents read.

Differences from California: costs per crash (as CA), but MnDOT's, SFY 2026; no published statewide base rates (CA
has TASAS); no person-level severity.
