# Client pain points (crash data)

The user's rule (2026-10-18): every data problem we find is a pain point our clients (agencies, consultants)
live with today. Keep each one here with **evidence**, **what it costs the client**, and **how CrashIQ addresses
it**. This is the source for client conversations, pitch material and the product roadmap. Add to it every time
a test or analysis turns up a new discrepancy.

| # | Pain point | Evidence (our measurements) | What it costs the client | How CrashIQ addresses it | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Most city-street crashes have no coordinates** | CCRS 2024: SF 35%, Sacramento 33%, LA City 56% have lat/long. SFPD ~1%, LAPD ~1% of their own reports | Engineers geocode by hand from street names, or drop crashes from the study | Automatic placement from the report's own clues (streets, distance, direction, address): 90% auto-placed at 98.8% in SF, the rest in a one-click review list | Built (PR #16) |
| 2 | **Officer GPS is not reliable either** | Long Beach PD: only 73% of records have GPS on the streets the officer wrote; some 446 km and 1,223 km away | Crashes plotted in the wrong place, hot spots shifted or missed, nobody notices | Self-consistency check: GPS vs the record's own street names; inconsistent points flagged for review | Measured; flag to build |
| 3 | **Street names written dozens of ways** | Real SF/LB records: `ALEMANY BL`, `07TH ST`, `MISSINO ST`, `CAN NESS AV`, `147TH ST (3100 BLOCK)`, `RT 101`, house addresses in the cross-street field | Pivot tables split one intersection into many rows; counts per location are wrong | Name normalization + matching to the real street network; every crash keeps its original text | Built |
| 4 | **The distance from the intersection gets lost** | ~9% of SFPD crashes: SF's own record has the distance, CCRS doesn't | Mid-block crashes counted as intersection crashes (wrong 250 ft assignment) | Use the distance when present (median error 2 m vs 38 m without); flag when missing | Built (flag to add) |

| 7 | **Duplicate and blank report numbers** | SFPD 2023–25: 25 blank/duplicate; one number used 4× for different crashes | Joins between tables or sources silently attach the wrong record | Uniqueness checks before any join; duplicates listed | Built |
| 8 | **Unit errors** | "Miles" offsets of 33–233 inside a city (feet typed as miles): 5 in SF, 146 in Long Beach | Crashes placed miles away, or dropped | Automatic fix with a flag on the record | Built |
| 9 | **Deleted reports still in the data** | CCRS has an `IsDeleted` flag (found by Hermes) | Counting reports CHP has withdrawn | Dropped on every pull, count reported | Built |
| 10 | **Property-damage crashes missing from common tools** | CCRS: 59% of 2024 rows are PDO; TIMS lacks most PDO | Patterns in non-injury crashes (rear-ends, sideswipes) invisible; EPDO weights incomplete | CCRS pulled directly, PDO included | Built |
| 11 | **Each city writes reports differently** | Long Beach drops directionals (`7TH ST` for East/West 7th), so the "relaxed" tier carries 44% of crashes vs ~2% in SF | One-size tools work in one city and fail in the next | Per-city validation before claiming accuracy; per-agency rules where needed | Ongoing (#17) |
| 12 | **Location clues buried in the parties table and narratives** | CCRS Parties has each driver's street, direction and movement; narratives are only in the PDF reports | Hand geocoders read them one crash at a time | Weighted-clue locator uses every structured clue; report upload reads narratives (planned) | Designed (#17) |
| 13 | **Some agencies leave key fields blank** | Each driver's street (Parties): Long Beach PD 0% filled vs 75% statewide (2024) | Tools that rely on the field silently fail in those cities | Per-agency field-fill profile; locator falls back to the clues that agency does fill | Measured |
| 14 | **Officer distances are rough estimates** | Only 58% of Long Beach distances are within half of the true distance (n=2,483) | Mid-block crashes land at the wrong spot or wrong side of the 250 ft line | Distance weighted lowest; beat and street names decide | Measured |
| 15 | **Overpass and underpass crashes leak into corridor counts** | 2021–2025 CCRS, Crenshaw Blvd under I-405 (1.65 mi): 95 crashes in the 2D buffer are on the freeway above (94 CHP IsFreeway, 1 written "RT 405" without the flag). | 16 | **The same corridor has a different name in the next city** | Union City Blvd becomes Ardenwood Blvd in Fremont; Fremont PD writes ARDENWOOD BL(VD), so a name search for the corridor missed 9 of 30 intersection crashes on the user's 1.6 mi study (Rancho Dr, Paseo Padre Pkwy) | Corridors that cross a city line lose the crashes reported by the second agency; nobody sees it because the totals still look plausible | Every OSM name and ref of the corridor's ways is searched and matched (Union City Blvd + Ardenwood Blvd; Nimitz Freeway = I 880) (#53) | Built (#53) |
| 17 | **The official data dictionary doesn't match the published data** | CCRS injury levels: the dictionary says "SUSPECTED SERIOUS INJURY", the table says `SuspectSerious`; 2016 reports mostly use retired levels (`SevereInactive` 13,528 vs `SuspectSerious` 11). Matching on the dictionary gave the user's Crenshaw study 0 serious, minor or possible injuries (131 "unknown") | Severity and KSI counts silently wrong; a KSI-based HSIP or safety-plan ranking misses every non-fatal serious injury, and the totals still look plausible | Both spellings and the retired levels mapped to KABCO (standard SWITRS crosswalk); unknown values listed in the caveats; tests on recorded live rows (#75) | Built (#75) |
| 18 | **Some agencies stop filing crash reports, and nothing says so** | CCRS 2016–2025 (#73): 61 local agencies filed under half their 2016–19 median in a year since 2021. TIMS shows the same gaps (Gardena 5–6 F+I a year): it's built from the same CHP records | A study in those cities shows almost no crashes and reads as "safe"; no tool warns | Per-agency yearly counts vs the agency's own history; warn on the study when a year collapses (proposal) | Measured |
| 19 | **Police GPS snapped to the intersection** | Of reports the officer placed > 250 ft from the corner, 56–63% sit on a point used by 10+ crashes in Santa Ana, Stockton, Rialto, Citrus Heights (2021–25); CHP ≤ 1.2% | Mid-block crashes counted as intersection crashes; segment problems hidden | Compare GPS with the report's own distance; flag snapped points (proposal, needs a user decision on which wins) | Measured |
| 20 | **The same crash filed twice** | 1,152 report numbers in CCRS 2021–25 carry 2+ Collision Ids with the same two roads (Fountain Valley 126, LAPD 108, LASD 95, Victorville 68); e.g. CHP 9630-2023-00084 = ids 1975974 and 1975975 | Crash counts inflated at exactly the spots that look worst | De-duplicate on agency + report number + roads (bug #81) | Measured |
| 21 | **Roads written as route numbers** | Laguna Beach PD (0.2% GPS) writes 24% of road fields as route numbers (SR-1, SR-133); Yuba City, Tulare, San Carlos similar | Crashes on state highways through towns go missing from name-based searches | Search the road's OSM ref spellings too (bug #83) | Measured |
| 22 | **No cross street on the report** | 15% of Santa Monica PD's street crashes (14% GPS) and 10% of Signal Hill's (2% GPS) say NOT STATED / PRIVATE PROPERTY or nothing usable | No way to place them on a corridor without GPS; tools silently drop them | Counted under their own reason (bug #82); user decides review vs exclude | Measured |

| 24 | **Placeholder words hide inside street-name searches** | CCRS 2024: SecondaryRoad is `NOT STATED` on 4,649 rows; squeezed it contains STATE, so a "State Street" search read them all (Santa Barbara study: 6,683 no-GPS reports from four counties, 6,136 of them junk). ## How to use this with clients
- Lead with the client's own city: run the coordinate-coverage and GPS-consistency checks on their agency first.
  The numbers (rows 1, 2, 5) are usually the opening.
- Every claim above has a reproducible script or query in this repo; keep it that way.
